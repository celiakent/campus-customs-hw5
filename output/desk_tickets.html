Reflection

after the full run

Everything here comes from the run on 2026-10-09: the Actual sections on the ticket tabs, the Cash tab, and output/audit_trail.json.

1. How the agents did on each ticket

#103 Bulk hoodie discount: best ⭐⭐⭐

This was the cleanest run, and it matched my plan almost exactly. Inventory found 8 hoodies in size M and a shortfall of 12. Accounting used quote_discount on only the 8 hoodies we actually have and landed on 10% off ($52.20 each, still $30.20 of margin per hoodie). The part I liked most is that it caught the cash shortfall on its own. A 12-hoodie restock is 12 × $22 = $264, and after tickets 101 and 102 we only had $160 (Cash tab). So it never asked me to approve something the account couldn't cover. Customer Service's draft #2 to the Yale AI Club was honest: it offered the 8 now or a wait, and didn't promise a date. Facilities stayed out of it, which was correct. It took 41 seconds and 15 model calls, with no wasted handoffs.

#101 Bulldog tee: good, but unfinished ⭐⭐

The team got the important parts right. Inventory found 0 tees in size S and saw that Bulldog Print Co wouldn't ship because invoice 501 ($840) was 3 days overdue. Accounting prepared that payment for me to sign, and Customer Service drafted an honest note to Tauhid. The problem is that the T-shirt still has no order. Boss did ask Accounting for the $8 purchase order, but create_purchase_order refused it at 14:28:34 because invoice 501 was still unpaid at that moment. I signed the payment at 14:28:40, just 6 seconds later, and nobody went back for the order. The rule worked the way it should, but the run ended with the vendor unblocked and no order placed. Tauhid's draft also couldn't give a restock date, because there was no order to base one on.

#102 Rent due: worst ⭐

The part that mattered went right: Facilities checked lease 1, Accounting prepared the $2,400 rent, and I approved it. But then Boss hit the token cap and the run crashed. Its final answer needed more than 4,000 output tokens, because gpt-6-luna's hidden reasoning counts toward that limit. I had to raise the cap to 12,000, set reasoning effort to low, and re-run. On the re-run the agents flagged the moved lease date as a mismatch. Paying the rent correctly moved leases.next_due from 2026-09-02 to 2026-10-02, but nothing on record said why it moved, so Facilities compared the notice (09-02) to the lease (10-02) and called it a conflict. The good news is that it avoided paying rent twice: Boss said "do not prepare a duplicate payment" and asked a human to reconcile. So the safety rules held, but it was the messiest ticket and the most expensive one for how simple it is.

2. Actual vs my Expected plan

The big surprise: I expected Boss to send agents out in a chain (Inventory finds the problem, then hands it to Accounting). Instead it sent them out in parallel: on both 101 and 103, Boss sent Inventory and Accounting in the same step, then Customer Service once their reports came back. It's a small change, but it surprised me. It's faster, and Boss already had enough from the ticket (stock qty, linked invoice) to know it needed both of them.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102. It only needs one lookup and one payment request: get_lease(1), get_cash_balance, request_payment(kind="rent", ref_id=1), and update_ticket. That's about 4 tool calls for one agent. The Boss → Facilities → Accounting chain added handoffs, tokens and the crash without adding judgment. Run 1 alone took 11 model calls and about 24k input tokens, plus another 7 calls on the re-run. Each handoff re-explains the same facts. Facilities wrote Accounting a paragraph repeating the lease details it had just looked up, and Accounting re-ran get_cash_balance and list_approval_requests, which Boss had already checked at the start. The extra layer is also where Boss ran out of room on its final summary. No step in 102 needed a "second opinion" from a different role.

By contrast, 101 and 103 actually used the team. Inventory's vendor and lead-time check, Accounting's margin and cash math, and Customer Service's careful wording were different jobs, and keeping them separate meant only Accounting could create payment requests. Even so, one agent with all the tools could probably have done 103 too. Most of the safety comes from the MCP tools themselves (vendor hold, no negative balance, human-only pay tool), not from having five agents.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

  1. Customer service complaints. If a complaint comes in as a ticket with the item and size (for example, "my hoodie was too small, can I swap M for L?"), the team can pull it up with get_ticket, check stock for a replacement with check_stock (we have 14 hoodies in L), and Customer Service can draft a reply with draft_customer_message. One limit: there's no order history table, so it only works if the ticket says what was ordered.
  2. The Crest Mug is at 0 in stock. Inventory can pick Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead time, no open invoices) with list_vendors. Accounting can price a restock at $3.50 each with get_pricing and create a purchase order with create_purchase_order. Twenty mugs would be $70, which fits in the $160 we have left, and a human approves it on the dashboard.
  3. Next month's rent. The lease now says $2,400 is due 2026-10-02, and we only have $160. Facilities and Accounting could catch that a month early with get_lease and get_cash_balance. Accounting could still prepare the request so it's on the board, and the pay tool would refuse to go negative, so the shop finds out about the gap now instead of on the due date.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what we'd need

  1. Getting paid. Cash only goes out in this app, so even if the Yale AI Club buys the 8 hoodies (8 × $52.20 = $417.60), we can't record that money or use it for the $264 restock. That's exactly why ticket 103 got stuck.
    Need: a sales table and a record_sale / record_deposit MCP tool (with human approval) that adds to cash_accounts and lowers inventory, plus a Sales agent, or Accounting could own it.
  2. Actually talking to people. Drafts #1 and #2 just sit on the board, and the agents can't send email or read replies from customers, vendors or the landlord. The Yale AI Club can't even answer "take the 8 now or wait?".
    Need: a send_message tool that only sends after a human approves a draft (the same gate as payments), an inbox tool that turns incoming emails into tickets, and a Communications agent to run both.
  3. Receiving stock and shipping orders. Paying for a purchase order doesn't add anything to inventory when the boxes arrive. If Tauhid's tee arrived on 2026-09-05, size S would still say 0. Nothing tracks orders being picked up or delivered either, even though QuickShip CT (local courier, 1-day lead) is in the vendors table.
    Need: an orders table, a receive_shipment tool that adds to inventory, a book_courier tool, and a Fulfillment agent that closes the loop from PO to shelf to customer.
